Subject: P1 result: the tail is there without the window, and the window makes it worse

Hi Dave,

the control I flagged in the last mail is done: the pure 16/16 arm, same
prompt, no window (--kv q4lm32, --short-read 6144, no STRATA_KV_WINDOW), same
6,144 positions (124,877...131,020), 1,015 s. The windowed run had identical
flags plus STRATA_KV_WINDOW=1/S=256/R=64, so the pair is clean.

KL(fp16 || arm), fp16's top-256:

    arm      mean      median    p99       max      mean no top 1%
    pure     0.001817  0.000010  0.003669  2.7261   0.000054
    int8     0.000847  0.000002  0.001506  2.4931   0.000021
    win      0.002108  0.000023  0.003214  3.2112   0.000075
    q4_0     0.001350  0.000015  0.002691  1.2348   0.000056

Paired: pure - int8 +0.000971 mean (648 better, 5,496 worse), -0.000096
without the 1 % largest differences. pure - win -0.000290 (4,274 better,
1,870 worse), -0.000856 without the top 1 %.

The q4_0 arm is the same prompt and build (6,144 positions, WMMA off, no
window): mean 0.001350, median 0.000015, p99 0.002691, max 1.2348, 0.000056
without the top 1 %. Against int8 it is +0.000503 mean (953 better, 5,191
worse) but -0.000518 without the top 1 %; against win it is -0.000758 (3,940
better, 2,204 worse), -0.001543 without the top 1 %. In the last 1,024
positions its mean is 0.003139 against int8's 0.004141, but that is one int8
spike at 130952 (2.49 against q4_0's 0.59); without the top 1 % int8 is still
better there (0.000115 against 0.000253). The mean gap against win is five
positions, though: 130952 alone is -2.62 of the -4.66 nats and the top five
are 48 % of the absolute difference. Offline, on 24,000 of the same blocks,
q4_0's MSE is 0.32 dB worse than q4lm32's (9.99e-3 against 9.29e-3, and q4_0
wins 41 % of the blocks), so the plain grid is not the better quantizer - the
mean is a tail statistic. What the pair does show is that the window costs:
pure to win is worse on the mean, the median (0.000010 to 0.000023), the bulk
(0.000054 to 0.000075) and the max (2.73 to 3.21), and win loses to pure at
4,274 of 6,144 positions.

Top-18 by pure - int8 (the P0a analogue, not the absolute rank): 130954,
130972, 130951, 124891, 130943, 127887, 128883, 130952, 130942, 126924,
130973, 128583, 128547, 130945, 128793, 128516, 128533, 126867. Overlap with
P0a: 12/18, top five carry 48.5 % of the positive gap, mean gap at P0a's 18
positions +0.254. For win - int8: 18/18, 48.9 %, +0.415.

So the tail is there without the window: pure has its own spikes (max 2.73),
its five worst carry 53.3 % of the total KL, and 12 of P0a's 18 positions
recur. It is not a read-path artifact. But the window amplifies it: at P0a's
18 positions the pure gap is +0.254 against win's +0.415 (1.6x). Pure is
better than win on the mean and in the bulk (14 % / 28 % lower), while win has
the better p99 and the better last-1,024 bulk, at the cost of worse extremes
(130952 3.21 against 2.73, 130973 1.27 against 0.60). The window trades
moderate tail positions for more extreme spikes; it does not help the tail
overall.

One position is bad in every arm: 130952 is 2.73 in pure, 3.21 in win, 2.49
in int8 and 2.88 in tag. An 8.25-bit arm with 22.9 dB lower vector MSE still
has 2.49 nats there, so that one is model/reference sensitivity, not
quantization. It carries only 3.9 % of the summed pure - int8 gap, so it does
not drive the mean.

Together with P0b (the KV error at the top-gap positions is normal), the tail
is the model's sensitivity to the 16/16 error level, not a localized codec
failure. The window adds to it, which means P0a overstated the pure arm's tail
by about 1.6x at those positions.

Decision unchanged: norm correction dead, no 16-byte format, no 18-byte build,
no P2, B1 closed. No default changed.

The open question is no longer codec against read path. If you want to push on
the tail, it is: more bits at specific cells, int8, or a different scaling -
each a new pre-registered question. Do you see a lever I am missing that does
not just lower the error everywhere?
