Subject: Where our 4-bit KV cache plateaus (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, one 16 GB
 consumer GPU) - I need one lever, not a menu

Hi Dave,

Short version: we ship an opt-in 4-bit KV cache for a 48-layer MoE hybrid on
a single consumer GPU. It works, it is 36 % smaller than our int8 default at
128K, and it has plateaued. Five measured context lengths, no point where our
pre-registered rule says the last idea carries; and the byte budget says a
larger exact window is arithmetically closed. The remaining error sits in the
4-bit bulk and in the cells immediately outside the exact window. I would
like you to name ONE concrete change to this cache that has a real chance of
reaching int8 parity at <=5.0 bit/cell on this specific model, plus the
cheapest measurement that would kill it.

Everything numeric below is measured unless marked (static).


1. THE SYSTEM
-------------

Strata runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next on one PC. Experts live in RAM, the KV cache
in VRAM, and an adaptive expert cache fills the rest of the card. The whole
point of compressing KV is to free VRAM for experts: at 128K, int8 KV costs
1,584 MiB, our window 1,016 MiB - 568 MiB freed, 416 more expert slots.

Measurement machine: Ryzen 9 3900X, 64 GB DDR4-3000, RX 9060 XT 16 GB
(gfx1200), Windows, ROCm/HIP 7.1 for the numbers below (runtime is now 7.2),
engine 0.1.47. Quality corpus: RULER complex-word-extraction (cwe),
teacher-forced, paired per position, KL against an fp16-KV reference over its
top-64 (or top-256) tokens, renormalized. 4K has 8 segments; 128K/256K are
one segment each with a moving-block bootstrap.


2. THE MODEL, AS FAR AS THE CACHE IS CONCERNED (static)
--------------------------------------------------------

- 48 layers; every fourth (3, 7, ..., 47) is a full-attention QSA layer - 12
  layers carry the KV cache this is about. The other 36 keep a fixed
  recurrent (GDN) state and no growing cache.
- 2 KV heads, head_dim 256. RoPE covers 64 of the 256 dims; 192 are not
  rotated.
- A QSA query never reads the whole cache: an indexer selects at most 2,048
  cells per query (plus block rounding; selection width min(n_kv, 2048+3)).
  The indexer has 4 query heads, 128-dim keys, and pools one key per 4 cells.
  Its store is separate fp32, never normed or rotated - about 192 MiB at
  128K, i.e. ~1.0 bit per KV value. We do NOT count it in the KV bit/cell
  budget, and compressing it is a different question with a different
  correctness contract.
- 24,576 experts total (512 x 48). The deployed 2-bit weight set loads
  ~33 GiB of experts into RAM.
- Context 262,144. There is an MTP draft layer; speculation does not touch
  the cache format.

Consequence: only 12 layers matter, and the selection is sparse and
data-dependent. Dropping cells or replacing the selection is not an option -
a cell the indexer selects that is no longer present is a silent failure we
treat as a bug.


3. WHAT q4lm8v IS
-----------------

Bulk. A Walsh-Hadamard rotation (256-point, orthonormal, self-inverse) is
applied to K and V before quantization; the query is rotated identically, so
scores are unchanged and the output is rotated back. The storage unit is
ggml's 18-byte block_q4_0: one fp16 scale + 16 bytes of nibbles = 32 values.
head_dim 256 -> 8 blocks -> 144 B per head per cell; 2 heads x K and V ->
576 B/cell = 4.50 bit/value. int8 is 1,056 B/cell = 8.25 bit (8 bits + 0.25
scale share).

Levels. We keep the 18-byte block and change only the nibble->value map. K
uses Max-Lloyd's 16-level table for N(0,1), V an 8-level table (that is what
the "8v" means - 8 levels on V, not 8 bits). Both tables are scaled by 32 and
rounded to int8, so they ride the existing int8 MMA path. Scales: max|x|/87
(16-level) and max|x|/69 (8-level). Measured on 498,688 rotated values: q4_0
MSE 2.938e-2, 16-level Lloyd 2.740e-2, least-squares scale 2.551e-2. The
level table is worth 0.30 dB, the per-block fit another 0.31 dB.

Exact-cell window. The first R=64 cells (sinks) and the most recent S=256
cells of each of the 12 layers are kept in fp16 in a resident side pool;
everything else is the 4-bit bulk. The pool is a ring of R+S+B rows, B = the
prefill chunk (capped at 6,144 by a scan limit). One row costs 2,048 B per
layer (2 heads x 256 dim x 2 B x K and V), plus 4 B tags. Rows carry the cell
they hold, so a stale row reads quantized, never as the wrong cell.

Byte model (validated exactly against two measured anchors):

    bit/cell ~= 4.50 + 16*(R+S+B)/L

At L=131,072, R+S+B=6,464 -> 5.289 bit/cell, 1,016 MiB, ring 158.9 MB
(~152 MiB). At 32K the same window is 7.66 bit/cell - still cheaper than
int8's 8.25; break-even is ~27,600 cells.


4. WHAT WE MEASURED
-------------------

cwe-4k, mean KL (lower is better): int8 0.01688, window 0.02090, plain 4-bit
0.03182 -> 73 % of the gap closed. At 2K the window is statistically
indistinguishable from int8 (t=-1.92, window ahead in 2/8 segments). Needles
6/6 at 32K and 6/6 at 128K.

At 128K (text T1, last 4,096 positions): window 0.00663, int8 0.00959, plain
0.01587 - the window BEATS int8 on that statistic. But int8 keeps the p99
(0.01655 vs 0.02977), the last 1,024 positions (0.00265 vs 0.00444) and the
whole segment (0.10715 vs 0.13312). A second text reverses the tail-mean sign
but keeps the p99/last-1,024/whole-segment ordering. So the deficit is not
uniform: it is a tail-quantile and whole-segment deficit, produced by cells
IMMEDIATELY OUTSIDE the exact overlay (256 of the last 1,024 are exact, 768
are 4-bit).


5. THE PLATEAU, IN ONE TABLE
----------------------------

We pre-registered the last idea as a pure READ POLICY: same ring, same bytes,
same selection - just read the resident fp16 copy whenever the cell tag still
names that cell (STRATA_KV_WINDOW_TAG=1). It is free (0 bytes) and it lifts
exact coverage at 128K from 8.55 % to 48.6 % of indexer selections. Quality,
paired tag - window (negative = tag better), 95 % CI:

    Point        delta        95 % CI                     carries?
    ---------------------------------------------------------------------
    4K decode    -0.00198     [-0.00458; +0.00061]        no
    32K          -0.01008     [-0.03335; +0.01238]        no
    64K          -0.00953     [-0.01877; +0.00049]        no
    128K         -0.00001     [-0.00066; +0.00069]        no
    256K         -0.000302    [-0.000905; +0.0000001]     no

The rule required CI excluding 0 AND |mean| > 0.0013 nats. At 4K the tag
reaches int8 parity (+0.00051 [-0.00068; +0.00170], top-1 48/48); at 256K
KL(int8||tag) is 0.000103. In between it buys nothing. Coverage counter at
128K: 8.55 % of selections land in R+S, 40.8 % in the current chunk, 50.6 %
outside; recall R 20.3 %, S 63.4 %; the outside share grows with horizon
(0 -> 3.4 -> 18.0 -> 50.6 %). Pushing S to 8,192 would be needed for ~52 %
coverage and would make the ring cost more than the product point. MORE
WINDOW IS NOT THE LEVER.

One confound we can quantify: repetition, not length, drives the 128K null.
Reading the same passage 32.8K earlier lowers KL(fp16||int8) by 7.6x (mean)
and flips the tag effect from -0.00743 (fresh) to +0.00176 (repeated,
significant). The 128K tail is a second reading; the 256K tail is a fourth.


6. ALREADY CLOSED - PLEASE DON'T SEND US BACK HERE
--------------------------------------------------

- Clipping after the Hadamard: worse at 2K (+34 % KL, t=-2.29).
- Hadamard on/off as a quality arm: tie at 2K and 4K.
- "K needs more bits than V": falsified on this model (t=+0.05, McNemar
  p=1.000). The 16/8 split is a byte/codebook choice, not a measured
  sensitivity.
- 3-bit base without the window: worse.
- R=8 sinks: too small. R=64 is the short-context knee.
- A larger fp16 ring: measured (512/128 at 4K) ties 256/64 (t=+0.02) and
  costs 7 MiB more at 128K.
- Eviction / dropping cells: not allowed (silent failure).
- Replacing the indexer selection: not allowed (changes the model).
- The S/R knee sweep: deliberately not run - the coverage counter says the
  positional overlay is the wrong place to spend bytes.


7. CONSTRAINTS ANY ANSWER MUST SATISFY
--------------------------------------

1. Opt-in only. The int8 default path stays byte-identical (dump-logits SHA
   gate).
2. <=5.0 bit/cell at 128K. The ring may spend 0.50 bit/cell: 4,096 fp16
   cells, or 7,943 with an int8 ring (8.25 bit/cell), or 5,461 at 12 bit.
   With S+R=320 that leaves B<=3,776 in fp16 - and the one measured small-ring
   arm (B=512) ran at 105 vs 528 tok/s prefill (-80 %). Prefill may lose at
   most 10 %.
3. Quality floor: cwe-4k paired mean vs int8 <=0 with upper 95 % bound
   < +0.001 and int8 ahead in at most 4/8 segments; 128K whole segment, p99
   and last-1,024 <= int8 on both texts.
4. One GPU, 16 GB VRAM, Windows/HIP. CUDA, SYCL and Metal were NOT built -
   the code is backend-agnostic by argument, not by measurement.
5. Every cell stays addressable; a promised exact cell that left the pool is
   a fault.


8. THE QUESTION
---------------

Given this geometry, what is the SINGLE change you would make - to the codec
inside the 18-byte block, to the ring tier, or to where the exact bytes go -
that has the best chance of moving the last-1,024 / p99 / whole-segment
statistics to int8 parity at <=5.0 bit/cell?

Please pick one, not a menu. For that one, I need: the mechanism in a
paragraph, the bit cost (bytes first), why it should move THOSE statistics
and not just the 4K mean, and the cheapest measurement that would kill it.
The axes we currently have on the list, with their costs, are:

    Lever  Change                                          Cost
    -------------------------------------------------------------------
    L1     16 levels on K AND V (same block)               0
    L2     a different 16-level table (same block)         0
    L3     per-channel K scale BEFORE the Hadamard         0.06 bit/cell
    L4     int8 or 12-bit ring tier instead of fp16        -7.75 / -4.0 bit
    L6     low-rank or sparse residual on the bulk (GEAR)  residual bits

If you think one of these is dead on arrival, say which and why. If you think
the 5.0 target is wrong for this geometry, say that instead - a documented
negative result is a valid deliverable for us.
